What binaural beats are

Headphones play a slightly different tone to each ear. Your auditory system perceives their difference as a beat: 245 Hz in one ear and 255 Hz in the other creates a 10 Hz beat percept.

The percept itself is well established. What remains unsettled is whether listening reliably drives EEG activity into that same band or consistently changes focus, mood, or relaxation. A 2023 review of 14 EEG studies found mixed results rather than a single clear effect.

Frequency starting points

6 HzTheta-range · slower. Try it when you want to unwind.
10 HzAlpha-range · Undertone’s relaxed-focus default.
16 HzBeta-range · faster and more alert-feeling.
40 HzGamma-rate experiment · very fast; the binaural percept may be less distinct.

These are adjustable starting points, not prescriptions. EEG-band names describe frequency ranges; they do not guarantee a matching mental state.

White, pink, or brown noise?

PinkBalanced and softer than white. A good all-around starting point.
BrownMore low-frequency energy. Deeper, rumblier, and usually less hissy.
WhiteEqual power per frequency. Brightest and strongest at masking higher-frequency sounds.

There is no universal best color. A 2024 meta-analysis found small attention-task benefits from white/pink noise in ADHD or high-attention-symptom groups, but not in comparison groups; it found no qualifying brown-noise studies.

Safety & evidence notes

Start quietly. Stop if the sound causes discomfort, dizziness, headache, or ringing. Undertone is not a medical treatment, and app volume is not a measured sound-pressure level.
